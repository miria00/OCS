# OCS figures

Each figure below is generated by `examples/make_plots.py` (competitor
overlays come from `examples/run_competitors.py` / `competitors_socp.py`, cached
in the `competitors_*.csv` files). Every OCS number is produced by the one
operator-native restart-PDHG engine in `ocs/solver/composite_pdhg.py`, in
float32, to a 1e-3 relative residual. Regenerate with:

```bash
python examples/make_plots.py
```

The two figures that carry the paper's technical contributions are **A**
(matrix-free termination) and **G** (matrix-free equilibration).

## A: validation battery (`A_validation_battery.png`)

**Claim.** The matrix-free certificate is trustworthy: on a battery of LP / QP /
SOCP / OT instances, OCS reaches the requested tolerance and its reported
primal/dual residuals and surrogate duality gap agree with an external solver.

**How.** 12 LP, 12 QP, 8 SOCP, and an OT instance, each solved by OCS and checked
against CVXPY/SCS (the harness in `ocs/cvxpy_harness/`, validation only). Columns
report objective relative error, and the matrix-free certificate `r_primal`,
`r_dual`, and `gap` (a feasibility-assumed surrogate); the two residuals are what
OCS actually terminates on. Reported as median **and** worst case, not
cherry-picked; no clipping.

**Reads off.** LP median 1.3e-3 / worst 2.5e-3 (`r_p` 9e-4, `r_d` 9e-4, gap
3.2e-3); QP 1.1e-6 / 5.3e-6; SOCP 6e-4 / 8.8e-4; OT obj rel-err 1.8e-4.

## A2: reliability battery (`A2_reliability_battery.png`)

**Claim.** Beyond a few hand-picked instances, OCS solves a *sweep* of problem
classes across sizes and seeds reliably, and agrees with an external solver.

**How.** `examples/reliability_battery.py` runs 72 instances over 8 classes (LP,
QP, SOCP, SDP, exp-cone, power-cone, quantile, logistic) × sizes × seeds and
reports two rates per class: **reached tol** (matrix-free residual < 1e-3, OCS's
own termination) and **agrees w/ ref** (objective rel-err < 1e-2 vs CVXPY, SCS or
Clarabel). Non-finite (rare float32 divergence) counts as unsolved and never
poisons the medians.

**Reads off.** Overall **99% reached tol, 96% agree**. 100%/100% on LP, QP, SOCP,
exp, power, quantile, logistic; SDP is the honest weak spot (89% / 67%, worst
4e-2; degenerate dense SDPs are the hardest case for a first-order method). This
also serves as the numerical diff-vs-SCS for the public cone term builders and the
regression losses.

## B: batched throughput (`B_binpack_throughput.png`)

**Claim.** For the "one operator, many instances" regime, batching a whole family
into a single GPU solve beats solving instances one at a time, and beats
formed-matrix LP solvers past a crossover.

**How.** Fractional bin-packing family (`ocs/operators/structured.py:BinPack`),
4,800 variables/instance (120 items × 40 bins). Baselines: OCS solved
**sequentially** instance-by-instance, plus cuOpt and HiGHS on the formed LP.
Two OCS-batched curves: one **monolithic** `solve_many` of all N, and one
**auto-microbatched** into tiles of 256. Timings are wall-clock, mean of 3 reps,
JIT excluded, per each solver's own accuracy setting. RTX 4090, float32.

**Note on the 1024 point.** A single monolithic batch of 1024 saturates the
device and throughput *falls* to ~27 inst/s there. Tiling the same workload into
microbatches near the throughput optimum (256) recovers **~43 inst/s**, both
faster and the correct production behavior (`solve_many` should pick a batch size,
not force one giant batch). The two curves make this explicit rather than hiding
the saturation; the microbatched curve stays above cuOpt (~35/s) at 1024.
(This supersedes an earlier "3.3× at 1024" claim, which came from a smaller
configuration and should not be used.)

## C: FFT wedge (`C_fft_wedge.png`)

**Claim.** On a subsampled-FFT least-squares family, the formed-matrix approach
runs out of device memory at a problem size where the operator-native solver is
still cheap, a region competitors "cannot enter."

**How.** x-axis is signal length `n`; y-axis is wall time for a **fixed
500-iteration budget** (compares implementation scaling, not time to a common
accuracy; all OCS points finish below the requested residual). OCS applies `M F`
matrix-free. The green wedge is where a **CVXPY/SCS, explicitly formed dense
operator** exceeds device memory. Two distinct markers: the *estimated* size
where the explicit operator alone crosses 24 GB, and the separately *measured*
host-OOM point. The scaling claim is scoped to the tested CVXPY/SCS
canonicalization path, not all explicit solvers. Competitor solve-time points are
overlaid where they do run, extended to overlap OCS's range so the comparison is
not an extrapolation. RTX 4090, float32.

## D: MRI SOCP scale wedge (`D_mri_socp_scale.png`)

**Claim.** The same wedge holds for an SOCP: isotropic-TV MRI reconstruction
scales in OCS well past where a formed SOCP is canonicalizable.

**How.** SOCP-representable isotropic-TV objective: **one grouped ℓ₂ prox per
pixel** (`Gradient2D` + `GroupL2`, size-2 groups, **2-D reconstruction**, pixels
not voxels), evaluated as **one batched prox per block type**. x-axis is image
size; y-axis is wall time for a fixed 4000-iteration budget (implementation
scaling, not time-to-accuracy). Green wedge is an *estimated lower bound*, where
the explicit conic operator alone exceeds 24 GB. Competitor solve-time line
overlaid on the feasible range. RTX 4090, float32.

## E: grouped prox dispatch (`E_grouped_prox.png`)

**Claim.** The prox stage is flat in the number of blocks, and stays cheap across
heterogeneous mixes of block types, so thousands of blocks, and 1-8 distinct
types, are all handled in one batched dispatch per type.

**How.** Two panels. **(a)** identical blocks: grouped `vmap` dispatch vs. a
Python per-block loop as block count grows: grouped stays flat, the loop rises
(≈46× at 512 blocks; the loop won't compile much past there). **(b)**
heterogeneous: grouped runtime stays low across 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 distinct block
types at fixed total (0.010→0.013 ms) while the Python per-block loop stays high
(~0.22 ms). Protocol: JIT excluded, `block_until_ready`-synchronized, best of
5×100 reps.

## F: Kronecker storage (`F_kronecker_storage.png`)

**Claim.** Never forming the operator is the difference between `O(S²T²)` and
`O(S²+T²)` memory: the formed operator is impossible at sizes the factors handle
easily.

**How.** `vec(X) ↦ vec(A X Bᵀ) = (A⊗B)vec(X)` (`structured.py:Kronecker`). Plotted:
memory to store the **dense explicit `A⊗B`** (S²T² entries) vs. the two factors
OCS keeps (S²+T²). Dense crosses 24 GB at T≈1210; at T=2048 it is 4,092× the
operator's footprint.

## G: equilibration ablation (`G_equilibration_ablation.png`)

**Claim.** Diamond-Boyd-style stochastic matrix-free equilibration limits the
solver's sensitivity to row/column scaling: a robust, orders-of-magnitude win on
ill-conditioned operators, using only `matvec`/`rmatvec`.

**How.** Two operator families: (a) a column-scaled Gaussian NNLS (a validation
operator) and (b) a genuinely operator-native row-weighted FFT NNLS. Each panel
plots the residual after a fixed 6000-iteration budget (geo-mean over 5 seeds)
vs. an imposed conditioning range `R`, for scalar steps vs. SMFE
(`ocs/solver/equilibrate_sgd.py`). Scalar degrades sharply as `R` grows; SMFE
stays near tolerance. SMFE protocol: 80 outer updates, 2 probes each, setup
amortized across repeated solves. This figure backs the matrix-free
**equilibration** contribution.
